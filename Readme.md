HCI & UX – Week 1 Assignment

Introduction

Human-Computer Interaction (HCI) is the study of how people interact with computers and digital systems. It focuses on designing user-friendly, accessible, and efficient interfaces that improve the user experience.

User Experience (UX) refers to the overall experience a person has while using a product, website, or application.

1. Introduction to HCI and UX

HCI combines computer science, psychology, and design to make digital products easier to use.

HCI Design Lifecycle

The HCI design lifecycle includes the following stages:

1. Understand Users: Identify users, their needs, and their expectations.
2. Identify Requirements: Determine what the system should achieve.
3. Design Solutions: Create layouts, wireframes, and interface prototypes.
4. Build the Design: Develop the proposed interface.
5. Evaluate the Design: Test usability and identify areas for improvement.
6. Improve the Design: Make changes based on evaluation and feedback.

3. Usability Evaluation of a University Student Portal

Introduction

Usability evaluation helps identify potential problems in a website or application and suggests improvements to make it easier to use.

For this assignment, a heuristic evaluation approach was used to consider common usability aspects of a university student portal.

Evaluation Criteria

- Navigation and accessibility
- Consistency of interface elements
- Readability and visual design
- System feedback
- Ease of learning

Potential Usability Concerns and Recommendations

The following are potential usability concerns considered through general heuristic evaluation. They are not presented as confirmed defects in the live university portal.

1. Navigation Complexity

Potential concern: Students may find it difficult to locate important sections if menu labels are unclear.

Recommendation: Use simple labels and organize related options into meaningful categories.

2. Readability

Potential concern: Small text or crowded information may make content difficult to read.

Recommendation: Use readable font sizes, appropriate spacing, and clear headings.

3. Color Contrast

Potential concern: Insufficient contrast between text and background can reduce readability.

Recommendation: Use accessible color combinations with sufficient contrast.

4. System Feedback

Potential concern: Unclear error messages may make it difficult for students to understand what went wrong.

Recommendation: Provide clear error messages and helpful instructions.

5. Dashboard Organization

Potential concern: Displaying too much information together may make the interface confusing.

Recommendation: Organize important information into separate sections and use a clean layout.

HCI Principles Applied

- Visibility: Important information and system status should be easy to identify.
- Consistency: Similar elements should behave and appear consistently.
- Feedback: Users should receive clear responses after performing actions.
- Accessibility: Interfaces should be usable by people with different needs.
- Simplicity: Unnecessary complexity should be avoided.
